How it works

Six easy steps. About one minute.

EchoLink sends the sound from your computer to your phone, so your phone becomes a speaker. You do not need an account or an app. Just follow the steps in order.

1

Open EchoLink on your computer

Open this page on your computer using Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge. These two browsers can share the computer’s sound. Other browsers may not work.

2

Press the start button

Make sure the Wi‑Fi tab is selected, then press One phone – get a code. A small window will open and ask what you want to share.

3

Choose what to share

Pick Entire Screen, switch on Share system audio, then press Share. Nothing is recorded or saved. Only the sound is sent to your phone.

4

Find your 4‑digit code

A number with 4 digits, like 4821, now appears on your computer. This is your pairing code. It works like a secret key that lets your phone find your computer.

5

Open EchoLink on your phone

On your phone, open this same page. Type the 4‑digit code into the box and press Connect & listen. Both devices must use the same Wi‑Fi.

6

Enjoy the sound

When it says Playing, the sound comes out of your phone and your computer goes quiet. You can pause, resume or disconnect from the computer or from the phone.

Guidelines

Get the best sound.

Follow these simple tips for a smooth and clear connection.

Before you start

  • Connect your computer and phone to the same Wi‑Fi.
  • Use Chrome or Edge on the computer.
  • Charge your phone, or plug it in.
  • Keep the phone screen on and the page open while listening.

Volume

  • Use the phone’s volume buttons to make the sound louder or quieter.
  • Do not change the volume on the computer while sound is playing. It can make the sound too quiet or stop it.
  • Start with a low volume and turn it up slowly.

Controls

  • Pause stops the sound for a moment. Resume or Play starts it again.
  • Disconnect ends the link with the phone.
  • Reconnect tries the same code again.
  • Stop sharing turns everything off.

Ways to connect

  • Pairing code: the easiest way. Type 4 digits. It needs internet on both devices.
  • Only one phone can connect at a time.

If something is not working

  • The phone cannot find the computer: check the 4 digits and make sure the computer is still sharing.
  • No sound: make sure you ticked Share system audio, and that the computer is playing something.
  • Still stuck: press Stop sharing, then start again to get a new code.

Good to know

  • The sound arrives with a tiny delay, about 50 to 300 milliseconds. This is fine for music and podcasts, but you may notice it in videos.
  • Your sound goes straight from your computer to your phone. Nothing is saved or recorded.
  • Your computer stays silent while the phone is playing.
Limits & Tips

Know what to expect.

Here are the options you can change, the honest limits of how EchoLink works, and tips to get the best result.

Options you can change

  • Keep the code alive and reconnect automatically (computer): when on, the same 4‑digit code keeps working if the phone drops and comes back.
  • Reconnect automatically (phone): when on, the phone tries to join again by itself if the connection breaks.
  • Pause, Resume and Disconnect: available on both devices, and they stay in step.
  • Volume: use the phone’s volume buttons.

What you need

  • Computer: Chrome or Edge. Sharing the whole computer’s sound works best on Windows. On a Mac or Linux, the browser may only share the sound of one tab.
  • Phone or tablet: any modern browser, with the sound turned up.
  • Internet: the 4‑digit code needs internet on both devices.
  • Same Wi‑Fi: both devices must be on the same network. Some public or guest Wi‑Fi blocks devices from seeing each other.

Connection limits

  • Only one phone can connect at a time.
  • The 4‑digit code stops working when you stop sharing or close the page.
  • A very strict network or firewall can block the connection.

Sound limits

  • There is a small delay, usually 50 to 300 milliseconds. It is fine for music and podcasts. In videos, the sound may arrive slightly late.
  • Bluetooth speakers or earbuds connected to the phone add extra delay.
  • EchoLink sends sound only. It does not send video, and it does not carry the phone’s microphone back to the computer.
  • Changing the computer’s volume while sound plays can make the sound too quiet. Use the phone’s volume.

When the phone screen turns off

  • On Android with Chrome, the sound should keep playing, and you get play and pause buttons on the lock screen.
  • On iPhone, Apple often stops web sound when the screen locks. This is an iPhone limit that a web page cannot change.
  • Battery saver or “sleeping apps” can still stop the sound. Set your browser to Unrestricted in the phone’s battery settings.
  • Keep the EchoLink page open in the browser. Do not close it.

The sharing bar on the computer

  • Chrome and Edge always show a bar saying the page is sharing your screen and audio. A web page cannot remove it. You can press Hide on that bar.
  • No picture is recorded or sent. Only the sound goes to your phone.
  • The EchoLink Desktop app has no browser bar. Use it if the bar bothers you.

Privacy

  • Sound goes directly from your computer to your phone.
  • The pairing service only helps the devices find each other with the 4‑digit code. It never receives your sound.
  • Nothing is saved, recorded or uploaded.
  • Stop sharing when you are done, so nobody else can join with the code.
